# Part 1 — Understanding what you've got

## 1. What Shopify is, and what your website is

**Shopify** is the company that runs your shop's engine. You pay them a monthly
fee, and in return they handle all the difficult parts: keeping the website
online, taking card payments safely, recording orders, tracking stock, and
sending confirmation emails. You never have to think about any of that.

Your website is made of two layers, and it helps enormously to keep them
separate in your head:

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  YOUR THEME  — "Abimra"                  │
   │  The look. Fonts, colours, layout,       │
   │  where things sit on the page.           │
   │  Custom-built for you.                   │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     sits on top of
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  SHOPIFY  — the engine                   │
   │  Products, orders, payments, customers,  │
   │  stock, emails, security.                │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

A **theme** is the design of your shop. Most Shopify shops use an off-the-shelf
theme that thousands of other shops also use. Yours is custom — it was built
for this business, which is why it looks like a print studio rather than a
generic shop.

The practical upshot: **you can change almost everything yourself, without
anyone's help.** Words, pictures, prices, which products appear where. What you
can't change yourself is the *structure* — and you very rarely need to.

## 2. The two places you'll work

Nearly everything you do happens in one of two screens. Knowing which is which
will save you a lot of hunting.

### The Shopify admin — *the business*

This is where the shop's actual contents live.

- Products and their prices, sizes, colours and stock
- Orders that have come in
- Customers
- Your policies, shipping rates, payment settings
- Discount codes

Think of it as the stockroom and the office.

### The theme editor — *the look*

This is where you change what customers see on each page.

- The words on your home page
- Which photographs appear and where
- Headings, buttons and the text on them
- Which products are featured
- Colours

Think of it as the shop window and the signage.

### The rule of thumb

> **"What am I selling?" → Shopify admin.**
> **"How does it look?" → theme editor.**

A worked example. Say you want to change the price of a hoodie: that's *what
you're selling*, so it's the **admin**. Say you want the home page to say
"Designed by you, printed by us" instead of something else: that's *how it
looks*, so it's the **theme editor**.

## 3. Logging in and finding your way around

Go to **shopify.com**, click **Log in**, and enter your email and password.
You'll land on the admin.

Down the left-hand side you'll see:

| Menu item | What's in there |
|---|---|
| **Home** | A summary of recent sales |
| **Orders** | Every order, new and old |
| **Products** | Everything you sell. **Collections** is tucked under here |
| **Customers** | People who've bought or signed up |
| **Content** | **Files** (your uploaded images) and **Pages** |
| **Analytics** | Visitor numbers and sales reports |
| **Marketing**, **Discounts** | Campaigns and discount codes |
| **Settings** (bottom left) | Everything else — policies, shipping, payments |

**To reach the theme editor:** in the left menu click **Online Store**, then
**Themes**, then the green **Customize** button.

### A note about the two themes

In **Online Store → Themes** you'll see more than one theme listed. One is
marked **Live** — that's the one customers see. Others are drafts or backups.

**Always check you're editing the Live one** unless someone has told you
otherwise. The theme editor shows the theme's name at the top of the screen.

### How to tell what customers are actually seeing

Open your shop in a normal browser window (not the editor). If a **black bar**
appears at the bottom of the screen, you're previewing a draft theme, not the
real site. **No black bar means you're looking at the live site.**

This catches people out constantly: they make a change, look at the shop, and
think it hasn't worked — when actually they're looking at a different version.

# Part 2 — Before you open

## 4. The before-you-launch checklist

These are things that are currently unfinished or carry placeholder text. Work
through them before you start sending customers to the site.

### Must do — legally or practically required

- [ ] **Replace the company details in the footer.** They currently read
      "company no. 00000000", "1 Example Street, Manchester M1 1AA" and
      "VAT no. GB 000 0000 00". These are placeholders. A UK limited company
      must publish its real company number and registered office.
      *Theme editor → Footer → Company details.*

- [ ] **Write your policies.** Settings → Policies in the admin. Write at least
      **Privacy**, **Refund** and **Terms of service**.
      **Important:** the links at the bottom of your site only appear once the
      policy behind them has been written. This is on purpose — it means you
      can never have a link that leads to an empty page. If you can't see the
      "Privacy" link on your site, it's because the policy is still blank.

- [ ] **Check the delivery promise.** The basket page says pieces are
      *"dispatched in 2–4 days"*. Nothing enforces this — it's just words. Make
      it true or change it.
      *Theme editor → Cart → Small print.*

- [ ] **Check the note promise.** It also says *"We read every note before we
      print."* Same thing — only promise what you'll actually do.

- [ ] **Finish the About page.** Some sentences are still placeholders and
      **they show in red with a dashed underline on the live site**, on purpose,
      so they can't be missed. See [section 14](#14-the-about-page).

- [ ] **Set up payments.** Settings → Payments. Until this is done you cannot
      take money.

- [ ] **Set up delivery charges.** Settings → Shipping and delivery.

### Should do

- [ ] **Add your social media links.** The icons are switched on but have no
      addresses, so **nothing shows on the live site**. An icon that goes
      nowhere is worse than no icon, so the site hides them until you fill them
      in. *Theme editor → Footer → Social.*

- [ ] **Build your menu.** Online Store → Navigation. This controls the links
      across the top of every page and in the mobile menu.

- [ ] **Set a Product type on every product** (see
      [section 5](#5-adding-a-product-step-by-step)) — without it your product
      cards read "4 colours" instead of "Oversized tee · 4 colours".

- [ ] **Install the Shopify Search & Discovery app** (it's free, from the
      Shopify App Store). Without it, customers can sort your collection pages
      but can't filter them by size or colour.

# Part 3 — Products

## 5. Adding a product, step by step

**Products → Add product** in the admin.

1. **Title.** What it's called. This appears everywhere.

2. **Description.** The text on the product page. Also helps people find it
   through search.

3. **Media.** Your photographs. This is the part that matters most — see
   [section 7](#7-photographs--the-most-important-part).

4. **Price.** If you leave it at 0.00 it will display as **£0.00** all over
   your shop, so set it even if you're still deciding.

5. **Inventory.** Tick *Track quantity* if you want Shopify to stop selling
   something when it runs out. For print-to-order you usually don't.

6. **Variants.** If a product comes in several sizes or colours, add them here
   as options.

   > ⚠️ **Name your colour option exactly `Color`** — with the American
   > spelling. Several features look for that exact word, including the one
   > that filters photographs by colour. If you call it "Colour" or "Shade"
   > those features quietly stop working.

7. **Product type** (right-hand column, under *Product organisation*). Type
   something like `Oversized tee` or `Heavy hoodie`. This appears on the small
   product cards across your site. Leave it blank and the card just says
   "4 colours" instead of "Oversized tee · 4 colours".

8. **Tags** (same area). Two tags do something special in your theme:
   - `customizable` — shows a red **"Design your own"** badge on the product's
     card
   - `new` — shows a red **"New"** badge

   A product that's sold out shows a "Sold out" badge instead; that always wins
   over the other two.

9. **Theme template** (right-hand column, *Online store* section). This picks
   which page style the product uses — see the next section.

10. **Save.**

## 6. Choosing a page style for a product

Your shop has **four different product page layouts**. You choose one per
product from the **Theme template** dropdown.

| Choose | When | What the customer sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Default product** | An ordinary product | All the photos stacked in a column, scrolled down the page |
| **studio** | A product photographed from several angles | One big photo at a time, with arrows and a row of small thumbnails underneath |
| **studio-variant-filtering** | A product photographed **separately in each colour** | Same as studio, **plus**: pick a colour and the photos narrow down to just that colour's photos |
| **sb-product-designer** | Products customers personalise themselves | Same as studio, plus a box where the customer can type a special request that arrives with the order |

If you're unsure, **studio** is a good default for garments.

> **If a product's page suddenly looks wrong** — all the photos stacked when
> they used to be a carousel — the Theme template has probably been reset to
> Default. Just set it back.

## 7. Photographs — the most important part

Your shop's whole look depends on one thing: **garments photographed on a
transparent background.**

### What "transparent background" means

A normal photo is a rectangle. If you shoot a white t-shirt against a white
wall, you get a white t-shirt inside a white rectangle.

A **cut-out** has the background removed, so only the garment remains. Saved as
a **PNG** file, the removed area stays genuinely empty rather than turning
white.

### Why it matters here

Your site places garments on soft coloured backgrounds — sand, sage, blue,
straw — which cycle so a row of products never looks like one flat block. It
also puts a soft shadow under each garment so it looks like it's resting on the
colour.

- **A cut-out PNG** sits beautifully on the colour, with the shadow tracing the
  garment's actual shape.
- **A photo with its own background** appears as a rectangle sitting on the
  colour, with a rectangular shadow around it. It looks like a sticker.

**If you see a rectangular shadow around a photo, that's the site telling you
the photo needs cutting out.** It's not a fault.

### How to get cut-outs

- Shoot against a plain, evenly-lit background (white or light grey is easiest)
- Use a background-removal tool — Shopify has one built in, or Photoshop,
  Canva, remove.bg and many phone apps will do it
- **Save as PNG**, not JPG. JPG cannot store transparency.

### If you can't cut something out

You still have an option — see
[section 9](#9-two-optional-extras-for-product-pictures). You can tell the site
"this photo brought its own background", and it will fill the whole tile
neatly instead of floating awkwardly.

## 8. Naming photos so colours work

**This only matters for products using the `studio-variant-filtering` page
style.** Skip this section otherwise.

If you photograph a garment in several colours, the site can show only the
relevant photos when a customer picks a colour. It works out which photo is
which colour **from the filename**.

### The rule

Put the colour first, then an **underscore**, then anything you like:

```
Black_hoodie-front.png      →  Black
Black_hoodie-back.png       →  Black
White_hoodie-front.png      →  White
Bone-White_tee.png          →  Bone White   (hyphen inside a two-word colour)
```

Everything **before the first underscore** is read as the colour name.

- Capital letters don't matter. `black_tee.png` works the same.
- A file with **no underscore** is simply ignored — it makes no claim about
  colour. So `faded-red-tee.png` won't be mistaken for the colour Red.
- Use a **hyphen** inside a two-word colour, not a space.

### The safety net

**If a colour has no photos of its own, all the photos show.** Nothing ever
goes blank. This means you can apply the naming to one product at a time, at
your own pace, and the rest carry on working.

### The one catch

**Shopify cannot rename a file after it's uploaded.** To apply this to photos
you've already uploaded you'd have to delete and re-add them, which clears
which colour each photo was assigned to. It's much easier to name files
correctly *before* uploading.

## 9. Two optional extras for product pictures

These are both optional. Everything works without them. They need to be set up
once by your developer (or by you, following the steps below) before the boxes
appear on your product pages.

### Setting them up, once

In the admin go to **Settings → Custom data → Products → Add definition**, and
create these two:

| Name | Namespace and key | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Card image | `custom.card_image` | File → accept only images |
| Card style | `custom.card_style` | Single line text → *Limit to preset choices*: `Inset` and `Fill` |

Once created, both appear near the bottom of every product's page in the admin.

### Card image — "a picture only used on the grid"

Sometimes the photo that works on the product page isn't the one that works on
a small card in a grid.

Set **Card image** and that picture is used on all the small cards — the home
page, collection pages, search results — while the product page keeps its own
photos.

It's a separate field rather than just another photo on purpose: a photo added
to the product's normal Media would *also* appear in the gallery on the product
page, and the point of this is a picture that is only ever a card.

### Card style — "does this photo have its own background?"

Two choices:

| Setting | Use it for | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Inset** *(the default)* | A cut-out PNG | Space is left around the garment so it sits *on* the coloured background |
| **Fill** | A photo with its own background | The picture fills the whole tile, edge to edge |

**Why you have to tell it rather than it working this out:** there's no
reliable way for software to know whether a picture has a transparent
background. A PNG file *can* be transparent but doesn't have to be. So it has
to be told.

**Leave it blank and it behaves as Inset.** That's right for most of your
photos.

# Part 4 — Collections

## 10. What a collection is

A **collection** is just a group of products — like a shelf in a shop.
"Hoodies", "T-shirts", "Studio Originals", "Design your own".

A product can be in several collections at once. Putting it in a collection
doesn't duplicate it; it just files it in another place.

Shopify offers two kinds:

- **Manual** — you pick the products by hand
- **Automated** — you set a rule ("every product tagged `hoodie`") and Shopify
  keeps it up to date for you

Either works with your theme.

## 11. Creating one and getting it to show up

### Step 1 — Create it

**Products → Collections → Create collection.** Give it a title and a
description.

### Step 2 — Give it an image

Scroll down to **Collection image** and add one. This is what fills its tile on
your Collections page.

If you skip it, the tile falls back — first to the first product's photo, then
to a drawn garment shape on a flat colour. **It never looks broken**, but a
real photo looks best.

### Step 3 — Tick the box so it appears

Your Collections page doesn't list every collection automatically. It lists the
ones you've **marked**.

On the collection's own page in the admin, look for a checkbox called
**Show in grid** and tick it. That's it — the collection now appears on
`/collections`, with no further steps.

> **If you can't find that checkbox**, the one-off setup hasn't been done. Go to
> **Settings → Custom data → Collections → Add definition** and create a field
> with the name **Show in grid**, namespace and key `custom.show_in_grid`, and
> type **True or false**. The checkbox then appears on every collection.
>
> ⚠️ **Don't delete or rename that field once it exists.** The whole grid runs
> off it, and removing it makes your Collections page show a heading and
> nothing else.

### To feature a collection in the big cards at the top

The two large cards at the top of the Collections page are chosen by hand.

*Theme editor → Collections page → Abimra collections → Add block → Collection.*
Pick the collection, and optionally override its title, description and image.
You can have up to four.

### To hide a collection from the grid

*Theme editor → Collections page → Abimra shop by theme → Add block → Hide a
collection.* Pick the one you want hidden. You can add as many as you like.

This is useful for collections you use for your own organisation but don't want
customers browsing.

## 12. Changing the colours behind products

The soft colours behind your garments can be changed at three levels. Each one
overrules the one above it, so you can set a general rule and then make
exceptions.

```
        Theme settings          ←  the whole shop
               ↓
        A single section        ←  e.g. just the home page row
               ↓
        One individual card     ←  e.g. just this one garment
```

### For the whole shop

*Theme editor → Theme settings → ABIMRA.*

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Paper** | The page background colour |
| **Ink** | The main text colour |
| **Press red** | The accent colour — buttons, the headline word |
| **Card backdrops** | *Cycle the palette* (varied colours) or *One colour* |
| **Card backdrop** | Which colour, when set to *One colour* |
| **Product page backdrop** | Behind the big photo on a product page |
| **Contact shadow** | How strongly garments appear to sit on the colour. 0 removes it |
| **Space around card images** | Breathing room between garment and card edge |

### For one section only

Sections like *Customise apparel* and *Studio Originals* have their own
**Backdrops and shadow** group. Set **Backdrop** to *Cycle the palette* or
*One colour* to override the shop-wide setting just for that row. Leave it on
**Theme default** to follow the shop.

### For one card only

On an individual card, switch **Give this card its own backdrop** on, then
choose the colour.

> ⚠️ **The checkbox is what makes it count.** If you pick a colour but leave the
> checkbox off, nothing happens. This is deliberate: a colour picker always
> shows *some* colour whether or not you chose it, so without the switch every
> card would silently override your shop-wide setting.

### The colours to use

These are the six designed backdrop colours. Using others will work but may
look off:

```
#E8E2D4  sand      #DFE7DC  sage      #EDE3C9  straw
#E3EAF6  blue      #DCD9CF  stone     #FBE7E1  soft red
```

# Part 5 — Your pages

## 13. The home page, section by section

*Theme editor → Home page.* The sections appear in a list down the left. Click
one to edit it.

| Section | What it is | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| **Abimra hero** | The big opening screen | Sized to fill **exactly one screen**. Everything you switch on competes for that space, which is why the small line above the headline and the ratings row are optional and currently off |
| **Brand story** | The scrolling animation | Plays **once per visitor**. Someone returning lands on the final frame with a "Watch again" button. *Scroll length* controls how many screens of scrolling it takes |
| **Abimra marquee** | The scrolling text strip | Each item should be a capability ("Printed in our own studio"), not a slogan |
| **Abimra steps** | The three "how it works" cards | Tick *Invert this card* on **one** card only — usually the middle |
| **Designed and worn** | Customer photos with credits | Switch off *Show "Designed by" prefix* for a shot that isn't a customer design |
| **Customise apparel** | The scrolling row of category cards | Each card can point at a collection **or** a single product |
| **Studio Originals** | The scrolling row of products | Set to follow a collection, so new products appear by themselves |
| **Worn and reviewed** | Customer quotes | Invert the middle one only |
| **Closing band** | The dark band at the bottom | The same section is used on five pages — editing it here only changes the home page |

### Reordering and hiding

Drag sections up and down in the left-hand list to reorder them. Click the eye
icon to hide one without deleting it.

> ⚠️ **Be careful with "Add section".** The menu contains some sections left
> over from the original base theme that were never styled for your design. If
> you add one it will look like a different website. **Stick to sections whose
> names begin with "Abimra".**

## 14. The About page

This page needs finishing. It lives at **/pages/about**.

### The red text

Any wording left inside **[square brackets]** appears **in red with a dashed
underline — on the live website, not just in the editor**. This is deliberate:
a placeholder that looks like ordinary text is one nobody notices for a year.

**Currently still unfinished:** the founder's name, and the line explaining why
the studio started. The year (2026) and the town (London) have been filled in.

Find them under *Theme editor → About page → About: how it started* and
*About: say hello*. Delete the brackets along with the placeholder wording.

### The photograph

*About: how it started* wants a **landscape photo, roughly 2000 × 1600 pixels**
(a 5:4 shape). A photo of the studio, the press, or garments drying works well.

### If the page doesn't exist yet

1. **Content → Pages → Add page**
2. Title it "About"
3. On the right, under *Online store*, set **Theme template** to `page.about`
4. Save

The page's own text box stays empty — all the content comes from the theme
editor.

## 15. Contact, Returns and Search

### Contact — /pages/contact

Messages arrive by **email**, at the address set in
**Settings → Notifications**. There's no inbox inside Shopify to check — just
watch your email.

The sender's address becomes the **reply-to**, so you can simply hit Reply and
it goes to the customer.

> **There's no file upload on the form**, on purpose. Shopify's contact form
> can't accept attachments — a file box would look like it worked and silently
> drop the photo. Instead there's a line telling people where to email artwork.

Everything on the page is editable: the field labels, the subject dropdown
options, the small print, and the thank-you message.

### Returns — /pages/return-and-exchange

> **Note the address: `return-and-exchange`, not `returns`.** Every link in
> your site points there. If you create the page with a different address the
> links will break.

Four editable sections: an introduction, two comparison cards (what's covered
and what isn't), numbered steps, and a list of common questions.

The template to choose in the Theme template dropdown is called `returns`.

### Search

Three ways in: the magnifying glass in the header, a search bar inside the
mobile menu, and the search page itself.

**For live suggestions as people type:** *Theme settings → Search input →* tick
the search suggestions option.

**For filters** (by size, colour and so on) you need the free **Shopify Search
& Discovery** app. Without it customers can still sort results, but there's
nothing to filter by.

> **An oddity worth knowing.** If someone searches a word that matches nothing,
> Shopify sometimes fills the page with suggested products anyway, so the
> "nothing found" message doesn't appear. This is Shopify's behaviour, not a
> fault in your site. To see the empty state, search for a number like
> `7777777`.

## 16. The basket

*Theme editor → Cart.* Every word is editable — the heading, the button text,
the small print, the empty-basket message.

**Open it in the editor with something in your basket**, or you'll only see the
empty state and wonder where everything is.

### Things to know

- **Express checkout buttons are switched off** on the basket page, deliberately
- **Delivery costs and discount codes are added at checkout**, not here
- For personalised items, the basket shows **the customer's own design** by
  default rather than the plain product photo — someone who's just made three
  t-shirts wants to see their three designs, not three identical blanks. You can
  switch this to the product photo under *Card image for personalised items*
- The **Trust line** blocks are the small ticked reassurances under the total.
  Add, remove or reword them freely

### Adding to basket

When someone adds an item, a small card slides in at the top right confirming
it, rather than taking them away to the basket page.

If you'd prefer it to behave differently, that's
*Theme settings → Cart → Cart type*.

# Part 6 — Running the business

## 17. When someone places an order

1. **You get an email.** The customer gets a confirmation too, automatically.
2. **The order appears under Orders** in the admin.
3. **Open it** to see what was bought, the sizes and colours, the delivery
   address, and anything the customer typed in the notes.
4. **Print it, make it, send it.**
5. **Click "Fulfil items"** and add a tracking number if you have one. This
   automatically emails the customer to say it's on the way.

### Refunds

Open the order and click **Refund**. You can refund part or all of it. The money
goes back to the customer's card automatically.

## 18. Personalised orders and where the artwork is

For products customers have designed themselves, the order carries extra
information.

- **Look at the order's line items** in the admin. The customer's design appears
  as a link you can open and download.
- **If a product page had the note box**, whatever the customer typed appears on
  the order under **"Special requirements"**.

> ⚠️ **Download the artwork files promptly.** The design images are stored by
> the design app, not by Shopify. They can disappear long after the order was
> placed. Save a copy when the order comes in — don't rely on the link still
> working in six months.

# Part 7 — Staying out of trouble

## 19. Things that catch people out

**"I changed something and the website looks the same."**
You're probably looking at a different version. Check for the black bar at the
bottom of the screen — if it's there, you're on a preview, not the live site.
Also try a hard refresh (Ctrl+F5, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

**"My new collection isn't on the Collections page."**
You haven't ticked **Show in grid** on it. See
[section 11](#11-creating-one-and-getting-it-to-show-up).

**"The template I need isn't in the dropdown."**
Theme templates only appear in the admin once the theme is live. If you're
working on a draft theme, publish it first — or ask your developer.

**"My social icons aren't showing."**
Each one needs **both** the switch turned on **and** an address filled in. In
the theme editor you'll see dashed grey circles for the ones still waiting;
those never appear on the live site.

**"The Privacy / Terms link is missing from the footer."**
That policy hasn't been written yet. Write it under Settings → Policies and the
link appears by itself.

**"Buy it now doesn't show on product pages."**
It's switched off deliberately on every product template.

**"A product page looks wrong — the photos are all stacked."**
Its Theme template has been reset to Default. Set it back to `studio` or
whichever it should be.

**"There's a rectangle shadow around one of my product photos."**
That photo isn't a cut-out. Either remove its background, or set its **Card
style** to **Fill**.

**"The animation on the home page doesn't play any more."**
It's designed to play once per visitor. Open the site in a private/incognito
window to see it fresh.

**"My product card says '4 colours' but not what kind of garment it is."**
Set the **Product type** on that product in the admin.

## 20. Things to never change

A short list. Everything else is fair game.

1. **Don't set both theme fonts to a "system font."**
   *Theme settings → Typography.* Your site's entire design — fonts, colours,
   spacing, the lot — is loaded alongside the custom typeface. Choosing a system
   font switches all of it off, and your shop will look like a plain, unstyled
   page. If this happens by accident, change the font back and it will return.

2. **Don't delete the "Cart footer" section** on the basket page. It looks empty
   and pointless. It isn't — without it the basket stops responding when people
   change quantities, with no error message to explain why.

3. **Don't delete or rename the "Show in grid" field** under Settings → Custom
   data. Your Collections page runs on it.

4. **Don't rename your colour option** away from `Color`.

5. **Don't add sections whose names don't begin with "Abimra"** on the home
   page. They're leftovers from the base theme and aren't styled for your
   design.

6. **Don't judge how scrolling behaves inside the theme editor.** The editor has
   its own scrolling and constantly reloads sections. Always check the real site
   in a normal browser window.

## 21. When to call your developer

You can safely do all of this yourself:

- Any wording, anywhere
- Any photograph
- Prices, stock, variants
- Adding, removing and reordering products and collections
- Colours and the settings described above
- Reordering or hiding sections
- Orders, refunds, discount codes

Call your developer for:

- Anything that needs a **new kind of section** or a new page layout
- Changes to **how a page is structured**, as opposed to what it says
- Installing or removing **apps**, if you're unsure of the effect
- Anything involving **checkout**
- Anything that still looks wrong after you've checked the list in
  [section 19](#19-things-that-catch-people-out)

### Useful things to tell them

If you report a problem, these three details save a lot of time:

1. **Which page** (paste the web address)
2. **Which device** — phone or computer, and which browser
3. **Whether there was a black bar** at the bottom of the screen

---

## A final word

Nothing you can do in the theme editor is permanent. Every change can be undone,
and Shopify keeps a history of your theme's previous versions under
**Online Store → Themes → … → Version history**.

The one thing to be careful with is deleting — a deleted section takes its
settings with it. Hide it with the eye icon instead if you're unsure.

Everything else: experiment freely. You'll learn the shop much faster by
clicking around in the editor than by reading about it.
